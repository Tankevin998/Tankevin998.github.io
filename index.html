About Me

Hi! I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Vanderbilt University, advised by Prof. Thomas Beckers, and I expect to graduate in December 2027.

I build generative world models for physical AI: models that learn how physical systems move from only a handful of sparse observations, respect the underlying physics, and know when they are uncertain. My work combines diffusion models, Gaussian processes, and conformal prediction with port-Hamiltonian structure, and puts the resulting models to work in forecasting, model predictive control, and robot planning.

Before Vanderbilt, I received my M.S. in Electrical Engineering from Washington University in St. Louis (2023), where I worked with Prof. Yiannis Kantaros on the robustness of trajectory predictors and on LLM-based robot planning. I received my B.Eng. in Information Engineering from Sun Yat-sen University (2021), where I was one of 18 students selected for its inaugural Information Engineering Experimental Program.

I am open to research internships in physical AI, generative modeling, and robotics. My resume is available here; feel free to reach out by email or LinkedIn.

Research Interests

  • Physical AI: world models of physical dynamics, physics-informed learning, learning-based control
  • Generative Models: diffusion models for trajectory forecasting from sparse observations
  • Uncertainty Quantification: conformal prediction, out-of-distribution detection, calibrated forecasts
  • Bayesian Methods: Gaussian processes, Bayesian learning of port-Hamiltonian systems
  • Robotics: safe planning and control, LLM task planning, reinforcement learning
